# Follow-up tests for eight added papers

**Data session:** 21 October 2026 UTC, local capture 19:40–23:59 UTC.  
**Purpose:** Make the eight newly added papers falsifiable against the retained session data. Results are exploratory proxies, not strategy recommendations. The underlying report has broader data and execution limitations: [Literature trial](./LITERATURE_TRIAL.md).

| Paper | Test and null | Result on local data | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deprez & Frömmel, [*Are Simple Technical Trading Rules Profitable in Bitcoin Markets?*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.iref.2024.05.003) | Proxy rule test: use stored 1-minute trend / own-return lag sign to follow or fade, then compare costed markouts with matched short-only. This does not reproduce their 75,360-rule search or false-discovery / holdout process. | Fresh SQLite rerun: at a 5-minute hold, following the nearest prior 60-second return averaged −0.269% and fading −0.295% (n=1,674); at 15 minutes, follow −0.287% and fade −0.278% (n=1,674). Matched short-only baselines were −0.225% and −0.154%. Neither follow rule beat short-only. The sample takes one signal per symbol, then spaces entries by 15 minutes; the earlier report uses a slightly different lag/join protocol. | **Proxy run; no edge.** Full technical-rule search is intentionally not run on one session because it would select noise and cannot support the paper’s data-mining correction or out-of-sample validation. |
| *Bitcoin Arbitrage and Exchange Default Risk* (2025) | Cross-venue direction proxy: use the latest closed Coinbase BTC 15-minute return to choose long/short on local assets; compare with short-only. The paper’s actual mechanism additionally requires exchange trust/default risk and executable cross-venue prices. | Following Coinbase BTC return averaged −0.220% at 5m and −0.228% at 15m; fading averaged −0.344% and −0.336%. Same-point short-only was −0.223% and −0.147%. This is common-market direction, not an arbitrage portfolio. | **Price proxy run; arbitrage mechanism not identifiable.** No synchronized executable two-venue bid/ask, transfer flows, or venue-risk measure was retained. |
| Voigt, Hautsch & Scheuch, [*Building Trust Takes Time*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00595) | Data-availability / arbitrage-bound test: determine whether the capture contains timestamped BTC transfer latency, mempool fees, confirmation requirements, exchange BTC inventories, and executable venue quotes that could define a net arbitrage boundary. | Local records do not contain those blockchain and venue-trust inputs. Cross-venue midpoint and bar-direction checks exist, but cannot estimate settlement-risk bounds or transaction routes. | **Not testable with yesterday’s retained inputs.** No arbitrage P&L was fabricated from midpoint gaps. |
| Ruan & Streltsov, [*Perpetual Futures Contracts and Cryptocurrency Market Quality*](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4218907) | Funding-window spread proxy: compare local modeled execution costs during the captured final UTC hour before 00:00 funding with prior captured hours; verify whether post-settlement data are available. | Fresh SQLite rerun, one costed 5-minute outcome per symbol every 15 minutes: median modeled cost was 0.230% (19:00, n=196), 0.227% (20:00, n=304), 0.228% (21:00, n=398), 0.228% (22:00, n=400), and 0.227% (23:00, n=397). The final hour before 00:00 does not show a spread-cost increase, but the capture ends at 23:59:59, so there is no post-event observation. | **Pre-event proxy only; no funding-window effect established.** Modeled costs are not quoted spreads, and one settlement boundary cannot identify the paper’s listing/event design. |
| Liu et al., [*Liquidity Commonality in Cryptocurrencies*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2025.108187) | Point-in-time breadth test: sort on the median 15-minute change in other coins’ 10bp depth, then compare next 5-minute cost-beating move rates and directional net returns. | Contracting-depth tercile: 35.3% of moves exceeded estimated costs; middle tercile: 28.3%; expanding tercile: 32.4%. Contracting-depth long/short mean net returns were −0.367%/−0.110%; all remained negative. | **Breadth proxy run; no directional edge.** It is not the paper’s daily liquidity-commonality or weekday-seasonality model. |
| Brauneis et al., [*Bitcoin Unchained: Determinants of Cryptocurrency Exchange Liquidity*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2022.08.004) | Within-asset depth test: compare thin and deep own-symbol book quartiles so the result is not driven by comparing large coins with small coins; report move size, cost and cost-beating frequency. | Fresh exact-time SQLite rerun, one evaluation per symbol every 15 minutes: at 5m, thin/deep books (n=401/391) had median absolute moves 0.102%/0.090%, median modeled costs 0.224%/0.228%, and cost-beating rates 24.9%/24.3%; mean long/short returns were −0.286%/−0.213% versus −0.284%/−0.209%. At 15m (n=400/390), cost-beating rates were 34.5%/32.8%, with mean long/short returns −0.369%/−0.130% versus −0.321%/−0.175%. | **Local liquidity proxy run; no useful directional/no-trade separation.** Blockchain transfer fees and multi-venue liquidity history are absent. |
| Dyhrberg, Foley & Svec, [*How Investible Is Bitcoin?*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2018.07.032) | Clock-time execution proxy: compare hourly local move sizes, estimated costs, and cost-beating rates. The paper studies full-day BTC/USD quote and trade patterns across three venues. | Fresh SQLite rerun, one 5-minute outcome per symbol every 15 minutes: in 19:00–23:00 UTC, median absolute move / median modeled cost / share exceeding cost were 0.115% / 0.230% / 29.1% (n=196); 0.095% / 0.227% / 26.0% (n=304); 0.067% / 0.228% / 16.8% (n=398); 0.132% / 0.228% / 32.5% (n=400); and 0.109% / 0.227% / 25.7% (n=397). | **Partial-day proxy run.** The sample covers only late U.S. hours and cannot reproduce the full intraday pattern; modeled broad-universe costs are not directly comparable with historical BTC/USD effective spreads. |
| Karasiński, [*The Predictability of High-Frequency Returns in the Cryptocurrency Markets and the Adaptive Market Hypothesis*](https://doi.org/10.2478/ceej-2025-0003) | Adaptivity test would estimate rolling return predictability on repeated, non-overlapping windows and compare it with a fixed-efficiency null. As a limited proxy, reuse local 1-minute own-return sign tests. | Fresh 60-second return autocorrelation proxy: split the capture into 19:40–21:50 and 21:50–24:00 UTC, requiring adjacent one-minute observations and at least 20 pairs per symbol. First-half mean/median per-symbol lag-1 autocorrelation was −0.040/−0.053 (98 symbols, 10,865 adjacent pairs); second-half was −0.008/−0.011 (96 symbols, 10,949 pairs). The change is small and windows are one evening with dependent assets; this is not a rolling-efficiency or out-of-sample test. | **Proxy run; adaptive-efficiency claim not testable.** No reliable window-by-window or out-of-sample inference is possible from this session. |

## Run record

I decoded the saved Insights SQLite records read-only for 21 October: 146,342 research-feature snapshots, 562,569 evaluation records (532,860 with both long/short net returns), and 522 external-market snapshots. The fresh calculations above use only same-session labels, with non-overlapping 15-minute entry spacing where the holding horizon allows. I also audited source coverage: the local schema retains venue book/features and exchange derivatives fields, but no exchange default-risk rating, transfer latency, mempool-fee history, or cross-exchange transfer ledger. The BTC/USDT/Coinbase direction proxy above reuses the previously fetched historical candle test in the main report; the underlying external candle response was not retained for a byte-for-byte re-fetch. The cross-sectional peer-depth test uses the already completed result in the main report (1,038 outcomes); current exact timestamp reconstruction did not yield a sufficiently broad timestamp join, so I did not replace that result with the incomplete rerun.

## Readout

Five papers have direct local proxy tests; two have limited proxies but their paper-specific mechanisms remain unidentified; one cannot be tested because required point-in-time inputs are missing. No tested directional proxy shows positive post-cost expectancy. Liquidity buckets sometimes separate cost-beating move frequency, but not net direction. These tests are narrower than the original studies and should not be treated as replications.
