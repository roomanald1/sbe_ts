# External sources for trading research

Research note, 23 October 2026. This is a data-source assessment, not a claim that any source creates positive returns. No source or strategy was activated.

## What the existing evidence says

The one-day replay in [REPORT.md](./REPORT.md) did not find a profitable rule after its recorded cost proxy. Median recorded cost was about 21 bps, versus median absolute moves of 5 bps at one minute, 10 bps at five minutes, 15 bps at 15 minutes, and 14 bps at one hour. This makes selective abstention and slower signals more promising research targets than adding another short-term price indicator.

A limited same-day check of the locally captured BTC/ETH funding, basis and OI fields is documented in [YESTERDAY_EXTERNAL_TEST.md](./YESTERDAY_EXTERNAL_TEST.md). It found mixed descriptive relationships and no evidence to promote a feature.

This repository already has a meaningful amount of the obvious data: OKX trades and order books, CoinDesk RSS/crypto news sentiment, and a 60-second OKX derivatives/options poll. The latter stores BTC/ETH funding, open interest, mark/index basis, ATM implied volatility and 25-delta skew in Insights as ExternalMarketFeatures; the /signals/external-market endpoint exposes the history. Those fields are currently persisted as context rather than joined into the live signal score. The poll stamps records with local time after the requests return; it does not retain exchange event timestamps, and it polls once per minute. That limits precise latency and point-in-time evaluation. Record both source and receive timestamps before training on it. The default Insights JSONL file was absent during this inspection, so there was no local poll history available for a predictive-value check. Avoid paying another vendor just to duplicate these fields.

## Candidate sources

| Source | What it adds | Access and practical fit | Main caveat | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Actual venue fee schedules and execution cost data** | Account-specific maker/taker fees, rebates, instrument lot/tick sizes and minimum order constraints can move the break-even threshold directly. This is not a predictive signal; it can prevent paper results from assuming the wrong economics. | Use the authenticated, read-only OKX GET /api/v5/account/trade-fee endpoint for the exact current account/product maker/taker rates; the public fee schedule is only a fallback. Record the account tier and effective date used by the simulator. Model maker fills and queue/adverse-selection risk separately. | The replay uses recorded net returns and execution-cost estimates, but Outcome records do not retain the account fee tier, so old trades cannot be reliably repriced against today’s schedule. A published base fee is not an account-specific quote, and maker fees do not imply a fill. Cost savings alone may not bridge the measured 21 bps typical cost. | **0 — required baseline before any feature trial** |
| **Cross-venue perpetual markets: Binance USD-M and Deribit** | Independent OI, funding, mark/index and basis; venue divergence and synchronized OI/price changes may help identify leveraged positioning and venue-specific dislocations. Binance also documents 5-minute basis history (last 30 days); Deribit public ticker/book-summary endpoints expose OI and derivatives market context. | Public REST/WebSocket market data; start with BTC/ETH and poll/stream alongside current OKX features. No key required for public market data. | OI changes are not inherently directional; contracts/quote currencies differ. Funding and basis can stay extreme. Cross-venue differences may be latency, index or instrument artifacts. Binance basis retains 30 days and open-interest history one month; OI periods with no trades are omitted, so capture forward and treat gaps as missing data. | **1 — low-cost, quickest test** |
| **Hyperliquid public perp data and archive** | A large on-chain perp venue adds independent funding, OI, mark prices, trades and L2 context; its official docs also describe a public historical S3 archive of asset contexts and hourly book snapshots. This is a less conventional venue source with some publicly accessible history. | Public API and WebSocket expose perp asset contexts (including funding/OI) and books; archived context/book files are available through requester-pays S3. Potentially useful for cross-venue flow and weekend regime comparisons. | Archive updates are approximately monthly with no guarantee of timeliness or completeness; historical snapshots do not include every market-data type, and transfer charges apply. Venue-specific contracts and participant mix mean it should confirm a hypothesis, not stand in for the liquid market being traded. | **1a — optional independent venue** |
| **Public liquidation-event streams** | A burst of forced closes may reveal crowded positioning and short-lived forced order flow, including cascades that aggregate OI changes can hide. Bybit's public all-liquidation stream covers its USDT, USDC and inverse contracts and pushes at 500 ms cadence. | Subscribe to the official Bybit public WebSocket for BTC/ETH and aggregate liquidation notional by side over fixed windows. Record event and receive timestamps; compare the feature against the existing OKX/Binance price, book and OI context. | Venue coverage is partial, reported bankruptcy-price/size fields are venue-specific, and liquidation bursts often follow the move. Test only lagged windows against executable forward returns; otherwise this is a description of a move already in progress. | **1b — inexpensive event-study candidate** |
| **Perpetual trader positioning ratios** | Account- and position-weighted long/short ratios provide a crowdedness/contrarian feature distinct from funding and OI. A 2023 study reports that speculative retail net-short behavior predicts crypto returns, even controlling for attention, uncertainty, sentiment and prior returns. | Bybit's public market endpoint reports long/short account-holder ratios at 5m through daily resolution and documents history back to July 2020. Binance Futures exposes top-trader position/account ratios and taker buy/sell volume at short intervals; its ratio endpoints retain only the latest 30 days, so capture forward. Keep retail-account ratio, top-trader position ratio and taker imbalance as separate fields. | These are venue-specific populations, not total market positions. Account counts do not measure notional exposure, and top-trader selection/definitions differ. A ratio may be a lagging reaction or a crowdedness proxy rather than directional information. Compare lagged levels and changes against costed returns and OI/price, using timestamped records. | **1c — high-value low-cost test** |
| **Clock-phase futures order flow (quarter-hour boundary)** | A July 2026 preprint finds that Binance perpetual trade imbalance specifically at quarter-hour openings forecasts cumulative returns four to twelve hours ahead; analogous 1m/5m opening imbalance was much weaker. It suggests testing phase-specific flow and slower horizons rather than indiscriminately adding high-frequency features. | Binance public futures aggregate trades include trade time and buyer-maker side; Binance's official public-data repository describes downloadable historical aggTrades. At each UTC :00/:15/:30/:45 boundary, calculate signed notional imbalance over the first 10 seconds, then test 4–12h returns in a chronological holdout. Existing OKX trades may support a separate venue replication. | The work is a preprint and the authors' own forecast-directed boundary returns average about 0.5 bps gross—roughly one tenth of a standard-tier taker fee—so this is not evidence of a profitable standalone trade. The likely use is as a slow-horizon conditioning feature. Align timestamps carefully and do not select the boundary window after inspecting the same holdout. | **1d — unusual, clearly falsifiable** |
| **Cross-exchange funding carry (a strategy, not just a feed)** | When one venue persistently pays a materially higher funding rate than another, a delta-hedged long/short perp position can collect the spread while reducing outright BTC/ETH direction risk. A spot-long/perp-short variant can also collect positive perp funding. | Funding rates are public on OKX, Binance and Deribit; start with synchronized public endpoints and paper accounting. This is closer to a return mechanism than a directional predictor. The current AutoTrader is configured around paper OKX spot fees, so two-leg carry cannot be evaluated faithfully without adding venue balances, perpetual positions and funding cash flows to the simulator. | A BIS study of spot/futures cash-and-carry (related, but distinct from perp funding spreads) found carry averaged above 10% annually and could reach 60%, while high carry predicted future crashes; margin spikes and liquidations make the historical premium risky rather than guaranteed. Funding rates and settlement cadence can differ by venue and change before a payment; basis can widen; both legs can be partially filled; fees, borrow, collateral haircuts, transfer delays and venue/counterparty risk can exceed the carry. Compute actual cash flows per settlement event, not from a fixed annualized-rate shortcut. Not risk-free arbitrage. | **2 — test gross carry net of all leg costs** |
| **On-chain exchange flows, especially ETH and USDT** | A 2024/2025 preprint reports intraday predictive associations at **1–6 hour** frequencies: ETH net inflows negatively predict ETH returns; USDT net inflows to exchanges positively predict BTC/ETH returns. This is a less obvious source and matches the repository's weak 1–5 minute economics better than a faster headline signal. | Needs labeled exchange-wallet flows from a provider such as Glassnode or CryptoQuant. CryptoQuant currently lists on-chain API access on its Professional plan at $99/month, with one year of API history; its pricing page labels the license personal use. First verify business-use rights, metric definitions, exchange coverage, revision policy and point-in-time timestamps. | Preprint evidence is not proof of net tradable alpha. It uses one study period (2017–2023) and hourly horizons; its option-profit illustrations focus on selected extreme events, and a base case assumes zero slippage. Wallet-label errors, publication/data latency and unusually large reported regression magnitudes need independent replication. Raw chain transfers are not equivalent to exchange flows. | **3 — strongest novel research lead, but vendor-cost gate** |
| **Options surface and expiry positioning** | Term structure, skew and concentration by strike/expiry can inform volatility regime and whether directional trades are likely to be whipsawed around dealer hedging/expiry. | Deribit public API provides options instruments and ticker/summary market data. Some fields already come from OKX. Add another venue only if Deribit's deeper options liquidity gives incremental coverage. | Gamma exposure is not directly observable from open interest alone; dealer side is unknown. Options data is more naturally a regime/risk filter than a five-minute direction predictor. | **4 — test as a no-trade/size filter** |
| **Stablecoin supply and issuance/redemptions** | Daily net issuance and chain-level supply shifts can proxy broad crypto liquidity / risk appetite. | DefiLlama documents public stablecoin-list and historical-chart endpoints; the general free API needs no key. This is suitable for slow regime features and inexpensive exploratory analysis, not a live entry trigger. | Supply is not the same as deployable exchange buying power. Update frequency is too slow for 5–60 minute entries. | **5 — context feature only** |
| **Network activity, hashrate and search attention** | A 2026 cross-sectional study reports weekly predictive value from active-user/network-activity and hashrate factors, alongside Google-search attention. These are different from exchange microstructure and could help explain which assets attract persistent demand. | Coin Metrics provides network metrics through its API with metric-specific access tiers; Google announced an official Trends API alpha with consistently scaled data, daily through yearly aggregation, and a rolling five-year history. This is a weekly asset-ranking/regime study, not an intraday trigger. | The Trends API is still limited to selected alpha testers, so do not assume access; Trends measures attention rather than buying. Network metric definitions, chain coverage, and revisions vary, and hashrate is meaningful only for proof-of-work assets. Another 2026 study finds attention spills into volume/volatility but not returns at daily frequency, so the return claim needs careful out-of-sample replication. | **6 — promising slower cross-section, access gate** |
| **ETF flows / fund creations** | Daily institutional demand context for BTC/ETH; potentially useful for holding-period or position-risk conditioning. | Public issuer disclosures and third-party aggregators; timestamp the publication, not the date the flow represents. | Usually daily and sometimes revised or published after the US close. Weak fit for a continuously running short-horizon trader. | **8 — daily regime only** |
| **CFTC Commitment of Traders (COT) positioning** | Weekly CME futures positioning by trader categories can show broad institutional/commercial crowding. It offers a public, independent check against crypto-exchange perp sentiment. | CFTC publishes reports and downloadable historical data free. The current CME report contains Bitcoin, Micro Bitcoin, Ether, Micro Ether and SOL contracts. Use the report release timestamp and map the exact contract. | Weekly snapshot is stale for intraday decisions and describes futures positions rather than total crypto exposure. Category labels do not reveal intent; use as a slow regime/context feature, not a direction trigger. | **7 — low-cost weekly context** |
| **Token unlocks and exchange announcements** | Scheduled vesting cliffs, new listings, delistings and protocol events can explain idiosyncratic volatility/supply shocks for smaller assets; use as a catalyst avoid/trade window rather than generic sentiment. | Tokenomist documents an API-key-gated supply API; Token Unlocks also publishes calendars (check API access/pricing); exchange listing/delisting notices are often public RSS/web pages and are the primary source for their own events. Keep event announcement time and original source URL. | Schedules can change, transfers are not necessarily sales, and headline latency may already be priced in. Most useful for avoiding crowded entries around known events; sparse events make backtests easy to overfit. | **9 — niche catalyst filter** |
| **Macro/event probabilities and calendars** | Scheduled CPI/FOMC/jobs releases and liquid prediction-market probabilities may help avoid entering just before a volatility jump; event surprise can drive crypto beta. | Use official release calendars (BLS, Federal Reserve) as a free event-risk schedule. Prediction-market APIs can be explored for probabilities, with event and timestamp mapping. | Mostly a risk/volatility filter, not a directional crypto signal. Calendars are safer and easier to validate than scraped social chatter; prediction markets can have thin liquidity and changing contract terms. | **10 — useful guardrail** |

## Evidence checked

- BIS Working Paper 1087, [Crypto carry](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1087-crypto-carry), documents high spot/futures carry (averaging above 10% annualized in its sample, up to 60%) and finds high carry predicts future crypto crashes. It studies spot/futures basis cash-and-carry, which is related to but distinct from cross-venue perpetual funding spreads. Historical averages are not a forward profit promise.
- Chi, Chu and Hao, “Return and Volatility Forecasting Using On-Chain Flows in Cryptocurrency Markets,” arXiv:2411.06327 v2 (revised 1 Sep 2025). Abstract reports 1–6 hour findings and the ETH/USDT flow directions summarized above: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06327>. This is a preprint, not a peer-reviewed guarantee; inspect full methods and robustness before relying on it.
- Live API smoke-check on 22 Oct 2026: public Binance [open-interest](https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/openInterest?symbol=BTCUSDT) and [premium-index](https://fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/premiumIndex?symbol=BTCUSDT) endpoints returned open interest, mark/index prices, funding and next funding time; Deribit [public BTC-PERPETUAL ticker](https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/public/ticker?instrument_name=BTC-PERPETUAL) returned an exchange timestamp, open interest, mark/index, funding fields and top of book. This confirms these endpoints are reachable and contain candidate fields; a single current response says nothing about predictive value. Normalize contract units and distinguish funding fields/settlement intervals before comparing venues.
- Binance USDⓈ-M Futures market-data API: <https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data>. Its basis endpoint supports 5m intervals and returns at most 30 days; its open-interest history endpoint supports 5m periods but only the latest month, with no record for periods without trades. There is no long ready-made archive for a multi-year backtest. Market-data endpoints are public; respect the published IP rate limits.
- Binance open-interest history documentation: <https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/catalog/core-trading-derivatives-trading-usd-s-m-futures/api/rest-api/market-data#open-interest-statistics>. It returns period-end time, OI and USD notional; the one-month window and omitted no-trade periods require forward capture and explicit gap handling.
- Hyperliquid official [perpetual asset context API](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/Hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint/perpetuals) documents mark price, funding and open interest; its [historical data documentation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/Hyperliquid-docs/historical-data) describes monthly requester-pays S3 archives and explicitly notes possible missing or delayed data.
- Bybit public [all-liquidation WebSocket documentation](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/websocket/public/all-liquidation). The current page documents 500 ms pushes, all liquidations for supported contract types, side, executed size, bankruptcy price and exchange timestamps. This is stream-availability evidence, not a claim that liquidation data predicts returns.
- Bybit public [long-short ratio endpoint](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/long-short-ratio) documents 5-minute through daily account ratios and a query history reaching July 2020. Binance Futures' [top-trader position-ratio endpoint](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/coin-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Get-Funding-Info) reports top-trader position ratios at 5-minute intervals but only for the latest 30 days. Dunbar and Owusu-Amoako, [Predictability of crypto returns: The impact of trading behavior](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2023.100812), report predictive value in speculative retail net-short behavior; the accessible abstract does not establish a universally profitable deployable rule.
- The [Quarter-Hour Effect preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09426) studies six Binance perpetual contracts and reports quarter-hour opening signed-flow associations with 4–12h cumulative returns. Its own forecast exercise estimates only about 0.5 bps gross per boundary, below ordinary taker fees; treat it as a candidate conditioning variable, not an alpha claim. Binance's [official public-data repository](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data) describes historical aggregate-trade archives and the buyer-maker flag used to infer aggressor direction.
- Guidolin and Ionta, [Predictive sorting of cryptocurrencies based on fundamentals and sentiment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intfin.2026.102285), report weekly cross-sectional factors from active users, hashrate, and Google search trends. Google Search Central's [Trends API alpha announcement](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/07/trends-api) says the API offers consistently scaled daily-to-yearly data over five years but is restricted to a limited tester group. A separate 2026 study, [The role of attention, sentiment and uncertainty](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.najef.2026.102627), finds attention spillovers to trading activity and volatility but not returns at daily frequency; this is conflicting evidence and argues for testing attention as a volatility/liquidity filter first.
- OKX [Get fee rates API documentation](https://www.okx.com/docs-v5/en/#rest-api-account-get-fee-rates) specifies the authenticated read-only GET /api/v5/account/trade-fee endpoint and supports instrument-specific spot and derivative fee rates. No account credentials or private account data were accessed for this research. Public [fee schedule](https://www.okx.com/fees) is not a substitute for the account-specific rate.
- CryptoQuant [current pricing](https://cryptoquant.com/pricing) lists Professional at $99/month, 500,000 monthly credits and on-chain API access, with a personal-use license and one year of API history; the Basic free plan excludes on-chain API access. Pricing and entitlements can change, so recheck before purchase or business use.
- CryptoQuant metric guide for exchange flows: <https://userguide.cryptoquant.com/cryptoquant-metrics/exchange>. It defines inflow/outflow/netflow and explicitly warns that exchange address labels can be delayed or revised and that internal wallet transfers can look like flows.
- Deribit API documentation: <https://docs.deribit.com/>. Public market-data methods include instrument/ticker and book-summary data; options collection guidance describes mark price, implied volatility, volume and open interest.
- DefiLlama API documentation: <https://api-docs.defillama.com/>. Stablecoin endpoints expose historical supply; treat that as a slow-moving liquidity backdrop.
- CFTC Commitments of Traders reports: <https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm> and current CME futures report <https://www.cftc.gov/dea/futures/deacmesf.htm>. The report observed on 22 Oct 2026 covered positions as of 13 Oct and was updated 16 Oct, illustrating the weekly reporting lag; preserve the release timestamp.
- Tokenomist API overview: <https://docs.tokenomist.ai/overview>. The token supply/unlock API requires an API key; confirm access and pricing before treating it as a free source.
- Polymarket market-data API: <https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/overview>. Public market data includes event/market discovery, prices and order books; the docs announce the v1 retirement on 24 October 2026, so any prototype should target Data API v2.
- Coin Metrics API documentation: <https://docs.coinmetrics.io/api/v4/>. Broad network and market metrics are available through a mix of community and credentialed access; verify each metric's access tier and interval before making a vendor decision.

## Recommended experiment

Do not switch on a new trading rule. Run a forward data-capture and shadow-evaluation experiment:

1. Before comparing signals, capture the actual paper account fee schedule and executable bid/ask costs. Separate maker and taker scenarios; do not count a maker fill unless the order-book replay supports it.
2. Add Binance public perp context for BTC/ETH: funding, OI, mark/index basis, and price. Keep the current OKX observations and record exchange timestamps plus local receive timestamps. Resample features to 1m, 5m, 15m and 60m, with explicit staleness limits. In the same capture, collect Bybit account long/short ratio and Bybit liquidation events; capture Binance top-trader ratio and taker buy/sell volume where retention and endpoint limits permit. Mark reconnect gaps. Consider Hyperliquid as a later cross-venue replication source after a candidate shows promise on the more liquid venues.
3. Define only a few interpretable candidate features before looking at results: cross-venue basis spread; OI change paired with price direction; positioning-ratio level/change; and OI/funding extremes as a no-trade gate. Separately reproduce quarter-hour opening order imbalance and evaluate it at 4–12h horizons. Avoid a large combinatorial rule search.
4. Join each snapshot to executable long **and** short labels and full bid/ask/fees, then compare baseline vs augmented feature set using chronological train/validation/future holdout splits. Measure net return, turnover, drawdown, sample counts, and calibration. Keep an untouched future period; do not keep retuning against it.
5. Treat spot/futures basis carry and cross-venue funding spread as separate strategies from directional features. Prototype explicit spot/perp legs, settlement cash flows, borrow, fees, margin use, drawdowns and liquidation handling in an isolated simulator before paper deployment; do not feed annualized funding snapshots into the spot AutoTrader.
6. In parallel, price a labeled on-chain ETH/USDT flow trial. Only proceed if a provider offers timestamped history long enough to test out of sample at hourly horizons and the subscription cost is below a predeclared fraction of conservative estimated incremental net P&L. Start with ETH net exchange flow and USDT exchange net flow; do not use raw transfer totals as a substitute.
7. For attention and network fundamentals, run a separate weekly cross-sectional study; do not blend those slow, sometimes revised measures into the intraday trigger. Check Google Trends alpha access before planning an API integration.
8. Only promote a feature if its incremental out-of-sample net P&L survives realistic fill/fee assumptions and improves risk-adjusted results across more than one market regime. Otherwise keep it as dashboard context or discard it.

This repo has useful infrastructure to capture additional external-market records, but existing ExternalMarketFeatures are not currently proven as predictive features. The strongest practical next capture is Bybit account positioning plus timestamp-correct Binance/OKX derivatives context; it is public, can be archived immediately, and is distinct from the already tested basis/OI/funding fields. In parallel, the quarter-hour flow idea can be replayed from Binance's historical public trades without a new paid data vendor, but the reported gross edge is much smaller than fees. The on-chain-flow and weekly network/attention studies remain second-stage candidates because they require paid or restricted data and slower outcome horizons.
