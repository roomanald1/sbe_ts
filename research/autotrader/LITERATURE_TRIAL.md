# Literature-inspired intraday signal trials

**Run date:** 22 October 2026  
**Market session tested:** 21 October 2026 UTC  
**Purpose:** Check published crypto intraday hypotheses against retained local execution outcomes. Exploratory research only; no strategy or runtime state was changed.

## Summary

See the [paper-by-paper follow-up tests](./ADDED_PAPER_TESTS.md) for explicit hypotheses, local proxy results, and data gaps for the eight most recently added sources.

I reviewed 104 literature entries spanning intraday momentum/reversal, order flow and books, periodicity, carry/funding, cross-asset predictability, on-chain flow, news, technical patterns and positioning. I tested local microstructure, own-asset return lags, stored news votes, book-depth and score-magnitude gates, UTC-hour and Deribit DVOL volatility proxies, and cross-venue BTC price, taker-flow, funding and positioning where timestamps allowed, including a Binance cross-sectional settled-rate rank. Public external observations came from six venues (OKX, Binance, Deribit, Coinbase, Kraken and Bybit), plus a stale but point-in-time CFTC weekly BTC position report. Polymarket odds supplied a limited historical five-minute probability/markout check, but not historical bid/ask quotes; DeFiLlama, Tokenomist and on-chain vendor sources did not supply defensible intraday-joinable observations for this session. The minute-level candle-turn proxy was marginally less negative gross at quarter-hour boundaries, but its uncertainty and execution costs erase any practical edge. No tested crypto-market rule produced robust positive net expectancy. A fixed-UP Polymarket markout was slightly positive after nominal fees but before spread/slippage and rests on one short session; other small or post-hoc buckets improved on short-only but remained negative or too sparse to trust.

## Data and method

The Insights SQLite store has 146,342 synchronized research-feature snapshots from 19:40:04.999 to 23:59:59.579 UTC on 21 October. It contains 135,948 five-minute and 130,528 fifteen-minute evaluation records that day. The local capture therefore covers only the final 4 hours 20 minutes of the UTC day. Exact symbol/timestamp joins were used; no feature was forward-filled. For the candle-turn check, I additionally used the first saved 60-second outcome within ten seconds after each UTC minute boundary, then averaged assets within each clock minute before comparing quarter-hour treatment minutes (17 clusters) with other minutes (242 clusters). The evaluator supplies both long and short returns with its stored bid/ask and fee assumptions.

For directional features, take long when the feature is positive and short when negative. For the existing signal-score benchmark, follow or fade its sign. Retain at most one observation per symbol per 15 minutes, so selected labels do not overlap at either tested horizon. These are descriptive samples, not independent observations: same-market assets and regimes remain correlated. There was no training, tuning or untouched holdout within this one-session test. Results are arithmetic percentage points per selected trade, equal weight per trade and no portfolio-capital constraints.

A newly indexed article-in-press, Xue and Zhou’s *Diagnostic expectations and cryptocurrency returns*, reports negative one-hour return predictability across 319 Binance USDT pairs for following one- to four-hour returns. I ran a narrower matched local check: 65 USDT symbols, 200 non-overlapping samples (at least one hour between signal starts per symbol), nearest prior local price within 10 seconds of the one-hour lag, and the next one-hour local outcome. The one-session OLS slope of prior gross return against subsequent gross return was +0.58 (correlation +0.34), not negative; a simple follow-the-prior-hour-sign rule averaged +0.150% net, versus -0.622% fading and +0.496% matched short-only. This does not reproduce the paper and does not establish continuation either: it is only four late-session hours, common market moves dominate, and no clustered inference or out-of-sample selection was possible. [Xue & Zhou (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jedc.2026.105416)

To test own-asset momentum and reversal directly, I computed each instrument’s lagged return from its nearest earlier local reference-entry snapshot, requiring the prior quote to be within 10 seconds of the requested lookback. I used one trade per symbol per 15 minutes, and selected side from the lagged return sign. Across 1-, 5-, 15-, 30- and 60-minute lookbacks, follow/fade net means at a five-minute hold were -0.256%/-0.308% (n=1,695), -0.272%/-0.289% (n=1,693), -0.257%/-0.305% (n=1,593), -0.261%/-0.302% (n=1,493), and -0.238%/-0.326% (n=1,295). At a fifteen-minute hold they were -0.209%/-0.356% (n=1,692), -0.289%/-0.272% (n=1,593), -0.231%/-0.335% (n=1,592), -0.261%/-0.304% (n=1,493), and -0.212%/-0.353% (n=1,295). Matched short-only means were -0.229%, -0.245%, -0.220%, -0.226%, and -0.220% at five minutes, and -0.153%, -0.171%, -0.146%, -0.153%, and -0.128% at fifteen minutes. No tested own-return lag beat the matched short baseline. The short-side benchmark varies with the per-symbol sample retained at each lookback, so compare within each row; no one-day result establishes a persistent short edge.

I also replayed the stored per-asset trend components directly, matching each snapshot to its evaluation by exact symbol and timestamp. On the one-per-symbol-per-15m sample, following the 1m and 5m trend components averaged -0.230% (n=1,004) and -0.227% (n=1,229) at a 5m hold, versus matched short means -0.161% and -0.187%. At a 15m hold, trend-follow averaged -0.255% (n=1,000) and -0.235% (n=1,227), versus short -0.098% and -0.122%; fading 1m/5m trend averaged -0.224%/-0.271%, also worse. The 10-second momentum component follow rules averaged -0.221% (n=599) and -0.215% (n=590) at the two holds, again trailing short-only. This adds the stack’s actual trend features to the simple lag-return proxies above; none improved the matched benchmark.

For a cross-sectional relative-value check, I sampled USDT-quoted instruments (excluding stablecoin base assets) on 15-minute boundaries, taking the first local evaluation within 30 seconds for each symbol. At each of 15–16 timestamps, I ranked each coin’s prior return and equally weighted the bottom and top quartiles as separate long and short legs, using their recorded net outcomes. With a 5-minute lookback, contrarian bottom-long/top-short averaged -0.348% for a 5m hold and -0.359% for 15m; momentum averaged -0.155% and -0.143%. With a 15-minute lookback, contrarian averaged -0.244% and -0.321%, while momentum averaged -0.258% and -0.178%. Only 0–3 timestamps out of 15–16 were positive across these variants. This cross-sectional construction reduces common market direction but is not dollar- or beta-neutral, is not the paper’s rolling LASSO or a cointegrated pair strategy, and still loses after recorded costs. The small number of timestamps prevents inference.

The turn-of-the-15-minute-candle hypothesis was checked using evaluation timestamps. Mean net return averaged across long and short was -0.233% at five minutes for turn-minute observations (n=96), versus -0.220% at other sampled minutes (n=57). At fifteen minutes the figures were -0.235% (n=99) and -0.220% (n=54). This is not a faithful replication of the paper’s minute-level portfolio and is too short to test its reported anomaly; it shows no advantage here after costs.

I also checked whether book depth works as a volatility/no-trade gate, following the distinction in volatility-forecasting papers between predicting move size and predicting direction. To avoid comparing raw dollar-depth levels across unlike coins, each symbol was ranked against its own depth history; the bottom and top within-symbol quartiles each contain 399 observations. At five minutes, thin versus deep books had median absolute gross moves of 0.094% versus 0.091%, median costs of 0.227% versus 0.229%, and moves exceeding estimated costs 25.8% versus 24.8% of the time. At fifteen minutes those figures were 0.151% versus 0.130%, costs 0.228% versus 0.227%, and cost-beating moves 34.6% versus 35.6%. Existing-score follow returns remained negative in both buckets: -0.265% versus -0.277% at five minutes and -0.296% versus -0.277% at fifteen minutes. Within-asset depth therefore did not give a useful no-trade separation in this session. A pooled cross-sectional depth sort made thin books look much more volatile, but that largely compared small illiquid assets with large assets and did not survive the within-symbol check. A separate test inspired by recent crypto liquidity-network papers measured each USDT market’s 15-minute log change in total 10bp bid-plus-ask depth, then used the median change in other coins as a point-in-time broad-liquidity proxy. On 1,038 locally costed five-minute outcomes (one per symbol per 15 minutes), the contracting-depth tercile (median peer depth -5.5%) had median absolute gross move 0.165% and exceeded estimated costs 35.3% of the time; the middle tercile (median +0.16%) had 0.128% / 28.3%, and expanding depth (median +5.63%) had 0.131% / 32.4%. This suggests a same-session move-size association, but median costs stayed about 0.22% and every bucket’s mean long and short returns remained negative; contracting-depth long/short averaged -0.367%/-0.110% net. This is a coarse liquidity-breadth proxy, not the paper’s estimated network, and the buckets were formed on this one session.

For the one-hour cross-asset-momentum proxy, I increased the per-symbol embargo to one hour. Following the sign of BTC’s prior five-minute return across other instruments produced n=400, mean +0.061%, median -0.164%, and 38.0% positive outcomes after costs. The largest selected outcome was +23.49%; removing it reduced the mean to +0.003%. The same ETH-return proxy averaged +0.078%, median -0.162%, 38.5% positive, and +0.020% after removing its best observation. Both trail the same-point always-short average (+0.197%), and the negative medians and one extreme winner make these weak evidence, not an edge.

Following recent cost-aware forecast-filter papers, I also used the absolute signal_score as a rough confidence proxy and only traded at thresholds 0.5, 0.75 or 0.9. This is not a faithful replication because the score is not a calibrated expected-return forecast. At 0.75, following the sign averaged -0.088% for the 5m outcome (n=15; median -0.234%) versus -0.456% short-only on those same points; at 15m it was -0.180% (n=15; median -0.157%) versus -0.099% short-only. At 0.5 the averages were -0.243% (n=64) and -0.285% (n=66), worse than matched short-only at both horizons. At 0.9, three short signals gave +0.089% at 5m and +0.718% at 15m, entirely driven by one of three observations. The 0.75 5m bucket improves relative to short-only but still loses after costs and is too small to treat as an edge; this is a post-hoc illustration, not a selected operating threshold.

A simple no-trade/range-state check also used the evaluation-time ranging probability (cut at 0.65) to condition the existing score. At five minutes, fading only in the high-range bucket averaged -0.214% across 377 non-overlapping observations versus -0.276% for fading across the full sample; at fifteen minutes it averaged -0.234% across 379 versus -0.268% overall. The low-range fade buckets were -0.294% (n=1,318) and -0.278% (n=1,315). This reduces the loss descriptively but does not produce positive net returns, and the threshold was not selected on a separate training period.

### Clock-time volatility check

A recent ten-year Kraken study reports that Bitcoin volatility concentration has migrated toward US equity hours and follows daylight-saving shifts, so the clock relation is not fixed in UTC. I bucketed the local five-minute outcomes by UTC signal hour, retaining one observation per symbol per 15 minutes. The partial 19:00 hour had 201 observations; hours 20–23 had 400 each. Median absolute gross move / median estimated cost / share of moves exceeding cost were: 19:00, 0.093% / 0.234% / 25.4%; 20:00, 0.097% / 0.222% / 26.8%; 21:00, 0.069% / 0.216% / 18.2%; 22:00, 0.147% / 0.221% / 37.2%; 23:00, 0.115% / 0.217% / 29.2%. Mean long outcomes ranged from -0.241% to -0.437%, while short ranged from -0.124% to -0.323%; direction flips across these hourly buckets. This single late-evening session has no US-hours versus other-hours comparison, no daylight-saving event and only four complete UTC hours, so it cannot test the paper’s mechanism or justify a time gate. A newer study of Bitcoin volume around the 15:00–16:00 New York ETF valuation window says activity clusters around the fixing but hour does not predict return direction. On our partial overlap, BTC-USDT median 60-second trade count was 129 during 19:40–20:00 UTC, compared with 84.5 during 20:00–21:00 and 95 during 23:00–24:00; ETH showed 50, 55 and 70, respectively. The different BTC/ETH pattern and truncated fixing hour make this a descriptive activity check only, not evidence of a repeatable time-of-day edge. ### Transaction-activity filter

For the activity/volume hypothesis, I joined each selected evaluation to the exact feature snapshot and ranked its 60-second trade count within symbol. Zero-count ties enlarge the lowest-activity bucket. At a five-minute hold, the low- versus high-activity buckets had score-sign-follow net means of -0.346% (n=807) and -0.253% (n=325), while matched short-only was -0.260% and -0.185%; absolute gross moves exceeded the chosen-side estimated cost in 22.6% versus 30.2% of observations. At fifteen minutes, follow was -0.382% (n=826) versus -0.222% (n=322), short-only -0.174% versus -0.110%, and move-over-cost rates 31.4% versus 36.6%. Activity reduced costs and losses relative to the low-activity bucket, but every activity bucket remained negative and none beat matched short-only. This is a post-hoc gate in one falling-market slice; it does not test the paper’s bullish/bearish regime interaction.

### News sentiment replay

I found timestamp-matched CoinDesk RSS sentiment votes in 146,293 retained research snapshots and joined them to the same-timestamp evaluation records. One trade per symbol per 15 minutes, following the stored vote, averaged -0.225% net at five minutes (n=1,695; median -0.211%; 16.8% positive) and -0.191% at fifteen minutes (n=1,694; median -0.208%; 22.6% positive). Fading averaged -0.336% and -0.373%, respectively. The matched short-only means were -0.214% and -0.148%; sentiment follow therefore did not add value, while fade was much worse. Vote signs were bearish in about 90.7% of retained snapshots and the modal vote was just -0.02, so this was largely a weak bearish prior during a down session. The row stores the aggregate vote and headline count, not the titles, source publication times or receive times; I cannot audit the underlying event relevance, independently rebuild the score, or establish the same availability latency. Treat this as a replay of the stored score, not an independently validated news strategy.

The same within-symbol quartile check on the retained 1m volatility component gives only a weak no-trade separation. In 399 low- and 399 high-volatility observations, the five-minute median absolute gross move was 0.084% versus 0.131%, median costs 0.229% versus 0.228%, and the move exceeded cost 25.1% versus 32.1%; score-follow net means were -0.301% versus -0.265%. At fifteen minutes the median absolute moves were 0.147% in both buckets, move-over-cost rates 36.6% versus 38.1%, and follow net means -0.326% versus -0.292%. The 5m filter shifts the proportion of trades with gross moves above costs, but most still fail to cover costs; this is not directional alpha and remains a single-session quartile comparison.

### External derivatives context

The companion [external-feature test](./YESTERDAY_EXTERNAL_TEST.md) joins local OKX funding, basis and open interest plus Binance five-minute basis and OI history to yesterday’s outcomes. Correlations change sign or size across assets and horizons. Naive funding fade lost on BTC; apparent ETH gains coincided with the falling market and were below the roughly 21 bp median recorded execution cost. OKX coverage spans only 19:40–23:59 UTC; Binance basis and OI are retrospective full-day history. Neither establishes live-available latency.

I also retrieved Binance USDⓈ-M funding history for BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT for the date from its public [funding-rate history endpoint](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Get-Funding-Rate-History-of-Perpetual-Futures). During the locally captured 19:40–23:59 window, the latest 16:00 UTC rates were positive for both (BTC 0.004896%, ETH 0.008498% per funding interval), so a simple funding-fade rule is just short-only. On the BTC/ETH non-overlapping sample it averaged -0.200% at five minutes (n=153), -0.194% at fifteen minutes (n=153), and -0.090% at one hour (n=36), all net of the recorded execution assumptions. This adds another venue’s funding observation, but no variation within the observed window to distinguish it from short exposure; one evening cannot validate an extreme-funding strategy.

I broadened this to the latest Binance funding records available at or before the capture start for 50 matched USDT instruments, excluding stablecoin bases and tokenized equities. On each 15-minute boundary I formed an equal-notional price-only portfolio that went long the lowest-rate quartile and short the highest-rate quartile (12 names per side). It averaged -0.296% net at five minutes across 17 timestamps and -0.328% at fifteen minutes across 16 timestamps, versus same-point short-only at -0.142% and -0.054%. These short markouts did not include a funding payment, and most matched records were the 16:00 UTC settlement; this screens the directional price leg only, not realized carry. The funding archive was retrieved retrospectively, so historical publication latency is unverified.

Bybit linear BTCUSDT hourly candles provide a further price-source check. Their hourly return signs across the capture match Kraken’s XBT/USD signs at each fully completed hour; following the Bybit series therefore gives the same 5m and 15m outcome statistics reported in the Kraken paragraph. The agreement supports the interpretation that this was common market drift, while the identical sign sequence means these venue tests are correlated confirmations rather than independent alpha discoveries.

For a third derivatives venue, I queried [Bybit’s historical funding endpoint](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/history-fund-rate) for linear BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT. The latest settlement visible during the captured interval (16:00 UTC) was positive for both: BTC 0.0100% and ETH 0.008707% per interval. It therefore also prescribes the same short-only exposure as Binance in this window; the recorded outcome is already represented by the short-only baseline above, so this venue adds confirmation of the common funding sign, not a distinct signal or independent return sample.

The same 16:00 UTC observations give a crude cross-venue funding-carry check: Bybit paid 0.010000% versus Binance 0.004896% for BTC (a 0.005104% or 0.51 bp gross differential per settlement), while ETH’s spread was only 0.000209% or 0.02 bp. A delta-neutral position would be long the lower-funding Binance contract and short the higher-funding Bybit contract. The next settlement fell at/after the end of the local capture, and we lack synchronized derivative order books, actual account fees and leg-level fills; this is only an indicative rate spread, not realized carry P&L. Even if the BTC differential persisted, it is small relative to the local execution-cost proxy.

Bybit also publishes a five-minute long/short account ratio. Throughout its 19:35–23:55 UTC completed buckets, BTC accounts remained 57.33–57.90% long and ETH accounts 70.07–70.32% long. A simple contrarian rule therefore collapses to short-only for both assets in the entire captured interval. On the BTC/ETH local non-overlapping outcomes this is -0.200% (5m, n=153), -0.194% (15m, n=153), and -0.090% (1h, n=36), exactly the matched short baseline, so it adds no incremental positioning edge. Historical endpoint timestamps are bucket times; we did not independently verify when those records first became visible to traders. [Bybit long-short account ratio API](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/market/long-short-ratio)

I additionally queried Deribit’s public hourly funding history for BTC and ETH perpetuals for 21 October. Its last 8-hour interest rate was positive for ETH throughout the captured window; BTC turned slightly negative at 22:00 UTC. Mapping the most recent hourly rate to exact-time BTC/ETH outcomes and taking the contrarian side gave -0.214% (5m, n=153), -0.213% (15m, n=153), and -0.174% (1h, n=36), below the Binance funding-sign/short-only check at every horizon. Deribit’s hourly record adds venue coverage but only a few rate changes and one negative BTC hour; it supplies no robust funding edge. [Deribit public API response](https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/public/get_funding_rate_history?instrument_name=BTC-PERPETUAL&start_timestamp=1792540800000&end_timestamp=1792627200000)

Deribit’s public BTC DVOL history provides an external implied-volatility series at one-minute resolution. I fetched its 21 October 19:40–24:00 UTC candle history through the [documented volatility-index endpoint](https://docs.deribit.com/api-reference/market-data/public-get_volatility_index_data), then used only the last available reading at least one minute before each local signal time. On the one-per-symbol-per-15-minute sample (n=1,500 5m outcomes), a rising versus falling 30-minute DVOL change produced median next-five-minute absolute gross moves of 0.098% versus 0.106%; the move exceeded estimated costs in 25.3% versus 29.7% of observations. Mean long/short net returns were -0.325%/-0.247% while DVOL rose and -0.363%/-0.194% while it fell. The proxy did not separate cost-covering moves or improve direction; a single evening is inadequate for forecasting evaluation, and the result is pooled across correlated assets. This history was fetched retrospectively, and its live publication latency was not captured.

The [CFTC CME futures-only report](https://www.cftc.gov/dea/futures/deacmesf.htm) visible on 21 October reports positions as of Tuesday 13 October, published Friday 16 October. Non-commercial BTC positions were net long 2,534 contracts (17,320 long versus 14,786 short). Following that weekly sign maps to long; fading it maps to short throughout this session, so it cannot time intraday turns. On the local BTC-USDT samples, this constant long/short mapping averaged -0.220%/-0.180% at 5m (n=18) and -0.228%/-0.173% at 15m (n=18); the short side is the same-session BTC short baseline, not evidence of incremental CFTC information. This is only a stale directional mapping, not a proper rolling COT forecast.

I also fetched Deribit BTC perpetual five-minute candles from 19:30 to 24:00 UTC using its [public chart-data endpoint](https://www.deribit.com/api/v2/public/get_tradingview_chart_data?instrument_name=BTC-PERPETUAL&start_timestamp=1792611000000&end_timestamp=1792627200000&resolution=5). Following BTC momentum gave -0.296% and -0.306%. Same-point short-only was -0.215% and -0.143%. This is an external-venue price confirmation of the cross-asset and momentum proxies, but it failed versus short-only and remains a single falling-market episode.

Binance USDⓈ-M futures five-minute candles expose total base volume and taker-buy base volume through the public [Kline endpoint](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Kline-Candlestick-Data). I fetched BTCUSDT candles from 19:35 UTC through midnight and used the last fully closed candle’s taker-buy imbalance sign (2 × taker buys − total volume) and price-return sign to select long or short across local USDT pairs. Across 18 sampled quarter-hour timestamps and about 64 pairs per timestamp, flow-follow portfolio returns averaged -0.261% at a 5m hold and -0.297% at 15m, versus same-time short-only -0.147% and -0.067%. The prior-candle price-sign rule averaged -0.244% and -0.255%. This is one falling-market slice, with equal-weighted cross-asset buckets and no beta neutrality; neither external Binance feature adds value here. For BTC-USDT alone (n=18), the flow-follow means were -0.219% and -0.223% against short-only -0.180% and -0.173%.

As a second spot venue check, I fetched Coinbase BTC-USD 15-minute candles from 19:15 to midnight via its [public candle endpoint](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/api-reference/exchange-api/rest-api/products/get-product-candles). The sign of the last fully closed 15-minute BTC return, applied across the whole local universe, produced -0.220% (5m outcomes, n=1,694 across 101 symbols; median -0.211%, 15.6% positive) and -0.228% (15m outcomes, n=1,594 across 100 symbols; median -0.212%, 20.8% positive). Fading the same feature was worse (-0.344% and -0.336%). Static short-only averaged -0.223% and -0.147%, respectively. The 5m follow result is effectively flat relative to short-only; the 15m result trails it. This confirms the same-session market drift across another public price source, not incremental alpha. Coinbase documents that candle history can omit intervals with no ticks, so these are recorded bars rather than a guaranteed continuous series.

As a third independent price-feed check, I queried Kraken XBT/USD hourly OHLC data for the six completed hours covering the local capture. I used the last completed hourly candle return as the signal, and matched it to local outcomes without looking ahead. Across the full universe, following that return averaged -0.230% at five minutes (n=1,695) and -0.266% at fifteen minutes (n=1,694); the corresponding same-point short-only averages were -0.213% and -0.147%. The one-hour outcome sample (n=400) was highly sensitive to cross-asset dependence and a small number of large returns. Kraken therefore confirms that the broad move was visible across venues, but adds no evidence of executable alpha. The requested hourly bars came from [Kraken’s public OHLC endpoint](https://api.kraken.com/0/public/OHLC?pair=XBTUSD&interval=60&since=1792605600); this retrospective bar check does not validate receive-time availability.

I also checked the new quarter-hour periodicity hypothesis using local 5-second signed trade flow at quarter-hour timestamps as a crude proxy for the paper’s opening order imbalance. On non-overlapping one-hour labels, flow-follow averaged -0.297% (n=182) at quarter-hour starts and -0.363% (n=123) at other sampled times; fading averaged -0.166% and -0.100%, respectively. Short-only was +0.257% and +0.545% in these sparse, correlated buckets. These 305 trades cover very few distinct timestamps and use a different horizon/proxy than the study’s 4–12-hour signal. The apparent relative ranking is not investable evidence and did not beat short-only.

A concrete event candidate was LayerZero’s ZRO unlock scheduled for 20 October; ZRO-USDT is present locally. I tested Polymarket’s resolved BTC five-minute Up/Down markets for the 52 windows from 19:40 UTC through midnight, using its public [Gamma event API](https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/) for resolution/outcome-token metadata and [CLOB price-history endpoint](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/overview) for trades. Requiring a trade mark no more than 60 seconds before the target time left 39 midpoint observations and 48 observations at or before 60 seconds to resolution. At midpoint, average UP-token price was 48.73% versus 51.28% realized UP outcomes; classification accuracy was 69.2% and Brier score 0.182 versus 0.250 for a constant 50% probability. Near 60 seconds to resolution, accuracy was 79.2% and Brier score 0.135, but a rule buying whichever outcome had the higher quoted probability was approximately flat gross (+1.0 cent/share) and slightly negative after the published taker fee (about -0.01 cent/share). Always buying UP showed +2.6 cents gross/share at midpoint and +3.0 cents near close; after the published fee formula, those were +1.4 cents and +1.9 cents before spread and slippage. For ETH, 40 midpoint marks averaged 48.86% against 45.0% UP outcomes (72.5% direction accuracy; Brier 0.154); following the higher-odds side earned only 0.5 cents gross/share and lost 0.7 cents after taker fees. At 60 seconds to resolution, 48 marks gave 77.1% accuracy, but the same rule lost 2.9 cents gross and 3.9 cents after fees. These are post-hoc one-session markouts, not validated biases or crypto spot/perp signals. The CLOB history gives last traded prices, not executable asks, and markets settle against Chainlink TWAP, which can differ from our local venue quote. The published fee is shares × 0.07 × price × (1 − price) for takers. [Polymarket fee schedule](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364478-trading-fees).

Retrospective data availability does not establish that the strategy could have observed that data at the same latency. Missing series were not fabricated or replaced with current snapshots.

## Conclusion

No literature-inspired rule tested here shows robust positive net expectancy on 21 October. Most standalone features trail same-session short-only; a couple of small or post-hoc filters improve relative to it on selected samples but remain negative or have too few trades for a reliable conclusion. Evidence is limited by the late-day capture, recorded costs relative to short-horizon moves, serial and cross-asset dependence, and one-day regime selection. This rejects promotion on current evidence; it does not prove that crypto intraday edges do not exist.

The highest-value next step is collecting more synchronized days with exchange and receive timestamps, verified fee tiers, full executable quotes, and broader cross-venue order flow. On-chain and event-driven hypotheses need their own timestamped historical inputs before they can be tested honestly.
